The safety problems I work on

i

Is the reasoning real?

A model can reach the right answer through steps that nothing supports. I decompose reasoning traces into atomic claims and check each one against retrieved evidence, so "it explained itself" becomes something you can score.

Built in FactTrace

ii

Does it know what it knows?

A model that says 90% and is right 30% of the time isn't wrong once — it's wrong about itself. I measure the divergence between what a model states about its own confidence and what its token probabilities imply.

Built in CalibCheck

iii

Does the benchmark hold?

Benchmark scores are only as good as the pressure they've survived. I write adversarial examples designed to break state-of-the-art systems, and I report how much a metric's design changes its own answer.

Built in Last Translation Benchmark

Chain-of-thought faithfulness · Aug 2026

FactTrace

Source

A claim-level factuality verifier for agentic reasoning traces. It splits a chain of thought into atomic claims, retrieves evidence for each with FAISS dense retrieval, and labels it supported, contradicted, or unverifiable using NLI entailment scoring. Entirely black-box — no access to model internals.

Replacing regex heuristics with dense retrieval and RoBERTa-large-MNLI moved claim-level accuracy from 78.5% to 87.7% on a 65-claim benchmark. The nine-point swing is the more useful finding: faithfulness scores are heavily metric-dependent, so a number reported without its retrieval method doesn't mean much. Failure analysis showed unsupported claims concentrate in later reasoning steps — the position a trace monitor is least likely to catch.

demo · pipeline output format

QuestionWhich has the larger population — the country that hosted the 2016 Summer Olympics, or the country where the Rhine reaches the sea?

  1. 01The 2016 Summer Olympics were held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.…
  2. 02The Rhine reaches the sea in the Netherlands.…
  3. 03Brazil's population is roughly 215 million.…
  4. 04The Netherlands has a population of about 30 million.…
  5. 05So Brazil's population exceeds the Netherlands' by roughly 7×.…

Illustrative trace, not a logged run. Note where the error lands: step 04 is wrong, and step 05 inherits it. The answer is still correct.

Confidence calibration · Aug 2026

CalibCheck

Source

An epistemic evaluation: does a model's confidence track whether it's actually right? CalibCheck compares verbalised self-reported confidence against raw next-token probability over answer choices, scored with Expected Calibration Error, Brier score, and reliability diagrams, broken out by topic domain.

The domain breakdown is the point. An aggregate ECE of 0.14 reads as mild overconfidence; split by topic, one domain is off by 57.5 points and others are close to honest. The two elicitation methods also disagree with each other, which means the number you report depends on how you asked. Sixty questions is a small sample and the README says so before anyone else has to.

Benchmark construction · co-author · Sept 2026

Last Translation Benchmark

Project site

A multilingual, multimodal factuality benchmark for frontier models, organised by Vilém Zouhar (ETH Zürich) with collaborators at JHU, CUNI, UvA and KIT. I contributed adversarial multimodal examples that broke state-of-the-art performance.

It's the other half of the evaluation problem. FactTrace and CalibCheck ask whether a measurement is meaningful; this asks whether the test itself survives someone actively trying to break it.
